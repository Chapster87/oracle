# "I Know a Guy"

**What This Rule Does**  
This rule allows players to retroactively establish connections in the world through roleplay. It encourages improvisation, character history, and emergent storytelling—while keeping control firmly in the DM’s hands.

## I Know a Guy

Sometimes, your character doesn’t have a plan.  
They just… **know someone**.  
With this optional rule, a player may declare that their character knows a person relevant to the current situation, even if that person has never been mentioned before.  
This declaration represents coincidence, past experience, or a connection that simply hasn’t mattered until now.

## Declaring "I Know a Guy"

A player may invoke **I Know a Guy** at any time outside of combat, subject to DM approval.  
When doing so, the player must briefly define:

- A **name** (real or nickname)
- A **general role or identity** (merchant, guard, criminal, scholar, noble, etc.)
- A **loose backstory** explaining how they know this person

This description should be **short, flexible, and non-mechanical**.

## Limitations

- You cannot declare exact loyalties, resources, or outcomes.
- You cannot use this rule to bypass major plot points or invalidate established lore.
- The DM has final authority over whether the declaration fits the setting.

## The DM’s Private Roll

Once the player declares **I Know a Guy**, the DM secretly rolls **1d20** to determine how the NPC reacts upon encountering the character.  
The player does **not** know the result.  
The NPC’s reaction may be influenced by:

- Time since last contact
- Past conflicts or favors
- Rumors, grudges, or misunderstandings
- How dangerous or inconvenient the situation is

### NPC Reaction Table (DM Only)

| d20   | Reaction                                                                                                 |
| ----- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1     | Actively Hostile. They resent or despise the character and may seek revenge, expose them, or cause harm. |
| 2–4   | Hostile. They want nothing to do with the character and may refuse help outright.                        |
| 5–8   | Unfriendly. They recognize the character but are suspicious, bitter, or reluctant.                       |
| 9–12  | Neutral. They acknowledge the character but offer no special treatment.                                  |
| 13–16 | Friendly. They are glad to see the character and willing to help within reason.                          |
| 17–19 | Helpful. They owe the character a favor or feel genuinely loyal.                                         |
| 20    | Devoted. They deeply trust the character and may take significant risks on their behalf.                 |

## Frequency & Abuse Prevention

- A player may use **I Know a Guy** a limited number of times per campaign or arc, at the DM’s discretion.
- Repeated use may result in disadvantageous rolls, strained relationships, or escalating consequences.
- The DM is encouraged to **reuse NPCs** created through this rule to strengthen continuity.

## DM Guidance

This rule works best when the NPC becomes a real part of the world—not a one-off solution. A bad roll doesn’t mean failure; it means complications. A good roll doesn’t mean safety; it means trust.

# Potion Maxing

**What This Rule Does**  
This rule gives players a meaningful choice when using potions: drink quickly for uncertainty, or take time for guaranteed results.

> **DM Guidance**  
> Potion Maxing rewards tactical decision-making. In the heat of combat, speed matters. In moments of desperation or safety, precision matters.

## Potion Maxing

Normally, drinking a potion is a **bonus action** and requires rolling to determine its effects.  
With this optional rule, a character may instead use their **action** to carefully drink a potion, guaranteeing the maximum possible benefit.

## Using a Potion

When you consume a potion:

- **Bonus Action**: Drink the potion normally and roll for its effects as usual.
- **Action (Potion Maxing)**: Drink the potion as a full action and receive the **maximum possible result** instead of rolling.

This applies to **any potion** whose effects are normally determined by rolling dice.

## Examples

- A Potion of Healing restores **10 hit points** instead of `2d4 + 2`.
- A Potion of Greater Healing restores **20 hit points** instead of `4d4 + 4`.
- A potion that grants a rolled bonus, duration, or variable effect instead grants its **maximum listed value**.

> This rule does not affect potions with fixed, non-random effects.

## Restrictions & Clarifications

- Potion Maxing does **not** stack with other features that maximize healing or effects unless explicitly allowed.
- You must be able to take an action to benefit from Potion Maxing; reactions or forced consumption do not qualify.
- If a potion has multiple random components, the DM determines which values are maximized.

# Flavor is Free

**What This Rule Does**  
This rule encourages players to personalize how their abilities look, sound, and feel without changing their mechanical effects.

> If a description would create mechanical advantages, disadvantages, or confusion, the DM may ask the player to revise it.

## Flavor is Free

Spells, abilities, and actions do not have to look or sound the same for every character.

With this optional rule, players are encouraged to describe how their abilities manifest in ways that reflect their character’s theme, personality, or background—so long as the mechanics of the ability remain unchanged.

## What You Can Change

Players may freely alter the **narrative presentation** of their abilities, including:

- Visual effects
- Sounds and sensations
- Thematic elements (fire, frost, shadows, light, water, etc.)
- Personal flair or signature motifs

These changes are purely descriptive and do not alter damage types, ranges, durations, or other mechanical properties.

## Examples

- An ocean-themed character’s spells may sound like **crashing waves, whispering tides, or creaking hulls**.
- **Magic Missile** could manifest as glowing runes, spectral fish, crackling lightning, or shards of ice.
- **Shield** might appear as a translucent wall of water, swirling leaves, or a momentary distortion in the air.
- A divine spell may feel like warmth, pressure, or distant music rather than radiant light.

> If the spell’s description requires a specific visible or audible effect, that requirement must still be met—only its presentation changes.

## What You Cannot Change

Flavor Is Free does **not** allow changes to:

- Damage type
- Spell range or area of effect
- Duration or timing
- Saving throws or attack rolls
- Any interaction with other rules

## Table Expectations

- Flavor should support the tone of the campaign.
- Descriptions should enhance immersion, not slow play.
- Repeated or signature descriptions are encouraged.

> This rule is about expression, not optimization.

## DM Guidance

When players lean into flavor, reward it with attention and narration—not mechanical bonuses. The more a character’s abilities feel like _theirs_, the more invested players become.

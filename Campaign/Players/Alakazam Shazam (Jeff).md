## Backstory

### The Winter That Didn’t Kill Them

No one remembers the storm’s true name.

Only that it swallowed caravans whole.

Alakazam was found after that storm — not crying, not freezing, not even shivering. A human couple, Elara and Finn, discovered a child sitting in the snowdrift outside their farmhouse, frost curling gently around his small hands like affectionate mist. No footprints led to the child. No tracks led away.

Just snow.

The couple took the child in. They told everyone the same story: “A poor thing lost in the storm.” And since the child looked human enough — pale hair, bright eyes, a faint unnatural symmetry to their features — no one questioned it.

The child grew into someone calm. Structured. Disciplined. Law mattered. Promises mattered. Rules existed for a reason.

Because chaos kills.

Storms kill.

And someone, somewhere, must stand firm against them.

### The First Sign of Power

When Alakazam was about twelve, a group of older youths cornered a smaller child by the river. Alakazam stepped in, heart pounding — and the air changed.

The river froze solid.

Frost spiderwebbed outward from their feet.

The bullies fled screaming as rime crept across their boots.

From that day forward, whenever your Alakazam’s magic manifested, it was winter.

Always winter.

Fire spells emerged as shards of freezing wind.

Lightning crackled as splintering ice.

Shield spells shimmered like translucent glaciers.

Even healing felt like numbing cold sealing wounds shut.

Alakazam concluded one thing:

This is what I am.

Cold.
Controlled.
Precise.

### The Lawful Heart

Raised by hardworking farmers, your character believes deeply in order and protection.

They intervene when they see injustice.

They cannot ignore cruelty.

They believe the strong must defend the weak — not dominate them.

They follow local laws unless those laws clearly harm the innocent.

They see their magic not as divine blessing, but as a tool. A weapon forged by fate.

If they are winter, then they will be the winter that protects — the frost that preserves rather than destroys.

### The World Beyond the Valley

For years, Alakazam's world was the valley of Silverstream. His sense of justice was applied to local squabbles—a property dispute, a case of stolen goods. But one summer, a traveling merchant, his wagon battered and his spirit broken, took refuge in the village for the winter. Over the long, dark months, he shared stories from his travels across the wider world. Unlike the heroic tales Alakazam was used to, these were stories of everyday injustice: corrupt watchmen in a distant city, bandits preying on a remote trade route, a cruel lord who exploits his people.

The merchant wasn't telling one story, but many. This painted a picture of a world where suffering is widespread and accepted. He spoke of good people who are too afraid or too powerless to stand up for themselves.

Alakazam realized his sense of duty cannot be confined to the valley. The world is full of chaos, and he has a unique power to bring order and protection. He doesn't know exactly where he's going, but he knows he must go _somewhere_. He must lend his strength to those who have none.

After a heartfelt farewell to his adoptive parents, he set out on the road south, not with a specific destination in mind, but with a clear purpose: to find a place where his unique brand of protective winter is needed most.

### The Truth They Don’t Know

Alakazam's Aasimar heritage lies dormant, quiet.

Their celestial guide has not yet revealed itself — or perhaps it whispers in dreams of endless white fields and distant golden light beneath the ice.

The reason they only use ice magic isn’t a true limitation.

It’s psychological.

Their powers first awakened in fear — in a snowstorm, in a moment of protection. Their magic latched onto that emotional memory. Subconsciously, they associate:

Cold with control

Ice with safety

Winter with survival

Other magic exists within them — radiant light, warmth, thunderous authority — but their mind refuses to access it.

Because fire burns out of control.

Because lightning is chaos.

Because winter is safe.
